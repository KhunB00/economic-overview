How to read this page

The small grey labels beside some numbers come from these rules of thumb — widely used reference ranges, not forecasts. They describe where a number sits, never what happens next. When a label and your own reading disagree, trust the number and the date.

IndicatorWhat it measuresBands
VIXExpected swings in US shares over the next month.below 12 → complacent · 12–20 → calm · 20–30 → elevated · 30+ → stressed
Gold volatilityThe same idea, for gold.below 15 → calm · 15–25 → normal · 25–35 → elevated · 35+ → stressed
Oil volatilityThe same idea, for crude.below 30 → calm · 30–45 → normal · 45–60 → elevated · 60+ → stressed
Real 10-year yieldWhat a bond pays after inflation — gold's main headwind. The higher it goes, the more it costs you to hold metal that pays nothing.below 0 → very supportive for gold · 0–1 → supportive · 1–2 → neutral · 2+ → headwind for gold
5y5y forward inflationLong-run inflation expectations. The Fed wants this near 2%.below 1.8 → below target · 1.8–2.5 → anchored · 2.5+ → unanchored — a warning
2s10s curve slope10-year yield minus 2-year. Below zero is an inversion, which has preceded most US recessions — though sometimes by over a year.below 0 → inverted — recession signal · 0–1 → normal slope · 1+ → steep
High-yield spreadExtra interest risky companies pay over the government. Widening fast is the earliest sign of trouble.below 3 → complacent · 3–5 → normal · 5–8 → stressed · 8+ → distress
CCC spreadThe same, for the weakest borrowers. It moves first.below 8 → calm · 8–12 → elevated · 12–18 → stressed · 18+ → distress
Financial conditionsZero is average. Negative means money is easier than normal.below -0.5 → loose · -0.5–0 → easy · 0–0.5 → tightening · 0.5+ → tight
Financial stressZero is normal. Above zero is strain.below 0 → below normal · 0–1 → elevated · 1+ → high
Jobless claimsWeekly US layoffs. Under 250k is a healthy labour market; a sustained climb above 300k has marked past downturns.below 250 → strong · 250–300 → softening · 300+ → weakening
Core CPIInflation without food and energy. Target is 2%.below 2.5 → at target · 2.5–3.5 → above target · 3.5+ → hot
Core PCEThe Fed's preferred inflation gauge. Target is 2%.below 2.5 → at target · 2.5–3.5 → above target · 3.5+ → hot
UnemploymentThe level matters less than the direction — rises tend to accelerate.below 4.5 → tight labour market · 4.5–5.5 → loosening · 5.5+ → weak
